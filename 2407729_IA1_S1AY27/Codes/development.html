Personal Development & Financial Philosophy

Reflections on George S. Clason's The Richest Man in Babylon

Managing finances, spending prudently, and building good daily habits are just as important during university as learning to write software. This page reviews two core chapters from George S. Clason's book: “7 Cures for a Lean Purse” and “5 Laws of Gold”. It also examines how those principles apply to my student budget and my preparation for a software engineering career.

Chapter Analysis: The 7 Cures for a Lean Purse

In ancient Babylon, the city's wealthiest citizen, Arkad, was asked by King Sargon to teach forty citizens how to build personal wealth. Arkad laid out seven practical financial principles that still hold up today:

  1. Start Thy Purse to Fattening: For every ten coins earned, spend only nine. Saving at least 10% of everything earned creates initial savings that can grow over time. Saving begins by setting aside funds for yourself before spending on anything else.
  2. Control Thy Expenditures: Expenses naturally expand to match whatever income is available unless kept in check. Separating true necessities from everyday wants ensures regular spending never cuts into the money saved.
  3. Make Thy Gold Multiply: Cash sitting idle does not build wealth. True financial security comes from putting saved money to work in reliable, productive ways so it earns returns over time.
  4. Guard Thy Treasures from Loss: Protecting initial capital comes before chasing profits. Arkad advises against risky schemes that promise quick riches, recommending that people seek advice only from those experienced in handling money.
  5. Make of Thy Dwelling a Profitable Investment: Owning a home provides stability and redirects monthly living costs into a long-term asset rather than ongoing rent.
  6. Insure a Future Income: Everyone needs to plan ahead for retirement or unexpected hardship. Putting money into long-term savings or insurance ensures family support when working is no longer possible.
  7. Increase Thy Ability to Earn: Earning capacity grows as personal skills improve. Developing expertise, staying disciplined, and learning new tools make a worker's time and effort more valuable.

Chapter Analysis: The 5 Laws of Gold

The five laws of gold are presented through the story of Arkad's son, Nomasir. Arkad gave Nomasir a bag of gold and a clay tablet inscribed with the five laws, sending him away for ten years to prove himself. Nomasir lost his initial coins to fraudulent ventures because he ignored the tablet. Only after following the five laws was he able to build a lasting fortune:

  1. The Law of Diligent Accumulation: “Gold cometh gladly and in increasing quantity to any man who will put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings to create an estate for his future and that of his family.” Money accumulates steadily for anyone willing to set aside a tenth of their earnings.
  2. The Law of Productive Labor: “Gold laboreth diligently and contentedly for the wise owner who finds for it profitable employment, multiplying even as the flocks of the field.” Invested capital earns returns and builds value when put into legitimate businesses.
  3. The Law of Wise Council: “Gold clingeth to the protection of the cautious owner who invests it under the advice of men wise in its handling.” Seeking guidance from experienced investors prevents costly mistakes.
  4. The Law of Unfamiliar Ventures: “Gold slippeth away from the man who invests it in businesses or purposes with which he is not familiar or which are not approved by those skilled in its keep.” Putting money into unfamiliar businesses without understanding how they work usually leads to losses.
  5. The Law of Deceptive Promises: “Gold flees the man who would force it to impossible earnings or who followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers or who trusteth it to his own inexperience and romantic desires in investment.” Chasing unrealistic returns or following tips from dishonest promoters usually results in losing the invested capital.

Personal Alignment: Connecting Babylon's Wisdom to My Life

Reading Clason's book as a university student at UTech and an aspiring software engineer at Apple highlights several direct lessons for everyday life:

Pragmatic Student Budgeting

Student income from family support, part-time jobs, or bursaries is usually tight. Applying the 10% savings rule and controlling everyday spending requires separating real academic needs from impulsive wants. By avoiding unnecessary tech gadgets and keeping an emergency buffer, I reduce money stress and can stay focused on my computer science coursework.

Increasing Earning Ability through Technical Skills

The seventh cure (increasing your ability to earn) directly mirrors my academic goal of becoming a software engineer. Higher earning power comes from building solid, practical technical skills. Focusing my efforts on low-level systems programming, operating systems, and performance tuning gives me the specialized foundation needed for competitive roles at technology companies such as Apple.

Avoiding Speculative Schemes

The 4th and 5th Laws of Gold warn against unfamiliar ventures and get-rich-quick schemes. College students frequently run into high-risk cryptocurrency speculation, trading bots, and questionable online investment pitches. Clason's advice reinforces why I stay away from ventures I do not thoroughly understand, focusing my resources instead on practical education, dependable savings, and useful skills.

My Biggest Takeaway from the Book

My biggest takeaway from The Richest Man in Babylon is that building savings and personal discipline comes from everyday habits rather than sudden windfalls.

In both software development and personal finances, it is easy to look for shortcuts or immediate results. But just like writing reliable software one module at a time, financial security develops through simple, repeated actions: saving a tenth of income, keeping spending within reasonable limits, asking experienced people for advice, and improving professional skills. Learning how to manage modest student finances prepares a person to handle larger earnings responsibly later in life.
